Part 2: Quiz Answer Key

1. What are the three fundamental properties that define a vector norm? A function is a vector norm if it satisfies non-negativity (the norm is $\geq 0$, and equals 0 if and only if the vector is the zero vector), homogeneity (\|c$\mathbf{x}$\| = |c|\|$\mathbf{x}$\|), and the triangle inequality (\|$\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{y}$\| $\leq$ \|$\mathbf{x}$\| + \|$\mathbf{y}$\|). These properties generalize the characteristics of the absolute value of real numbers to higher-dimensional spaces.
2. How is the distance between two vectors, $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$, defined in the context of vector norms? The distance between two vectors is defined as the norm of their difference, expressed as \|$\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}$\|. Similarly, the "length" of a single vector $\mathbf{x}$ is defined as its distance from the origin, or \|$\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{0}$\|.
3. What is the definition of the p-norm, and what are its two most common specific cases? The p-norm is defined as \|$\mathbf{x}$\|_p := ($\sum_{i=1}^n |x_i$|^p)^{1/p} for p $\geq 1$. Its most common cases are the 1-norm (p=1), which is the sum of the absolute values of the components, and the 2-norm (p=2), also known as the Euclidean norm, which calculates the square root of the sum of the squares of the components.
4. Explain the Cauchy–Bunyakovsky–Schwarz (CBS) inequality in its vectorial form. The CBS inequality states that the absolute value of the scalar product of two vectors is less than or equal to the product of their Euclidean (2-norm) lengths, written as |$\mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{y}| \leq$ \|$\mathbf{x}$\|_2 \|$\mathbf{y}$\|_2. Equality is achieved if and only if one vector is a constant multiple of the other ($\mathbf{y} = \lambda \mathbf{x}$).
5. What does it mean for a sequence of vectors $\mathbf{p}^{(k)}$ to converge to a limit $\mathbf{p}$? A vector sequence converges to a limit if the norm of the difference between the sequence elements and the limit vector approaches zero as the index k goes to infinity ($\lim_{k \to \infty}$ \|$\mathbf{p}^{(k)} - \mathbf{p}$\| = 0). This is equivalent to saying that each individual component of the vectors in the sequence converges to the corresponding component of the limit vector.
6. What is meant by the "equivalence of norms" in $\mathbb{R}^n$? Norm equivalence means that the convergence of a sequence does not depend on the specific vector norm chosen; if a sequence converges in one norm, it converges to the same limit in any other norm. Mathematically, for any two norms \|$\cdot$\| and |$\cdot$|, there exist positive constants m and M such that m|$\mathbf{x}| \leq$ \|$\mathbf{x}$\| $\leq M|\mathbf{x}$|.
7. How is a matrix norm "generated" by a vector norm? A matrix norm is generated by a vector norm by taking the supremum of the ratio of the norm of the transformed vector to the norm of the original vector: \|$\mathbf{A}$\| := $\sup_{\mathbf{x} \neq \mathbf{0}} \frac{\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\|}{\|\mathbf{x}\|}$. This represents the maximum "amplification" or stretching factor the matrix $\mathbf{A}$ can apply to any non-zero vector.
8. Provide the specific formulas for calculating the 1-norm and the infinity-norm of a matrix $\mathbf{A}$. The matrix 1-norm is the maximum absolute column sum, calculated as \|$\mathbf{A}$\|_1 = $\max_j \sum_i |a_{ij}$|. The matrix infinity-norm is the maximum absolute row sum, calculated as \|$\mathbf{A}$\|_$\infty = \max_i \sum_j |a_{ij}$|.
9. Describe the sub-multiplicative property of matrix norms. For any two n $\times n$ matrices $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$, the norm of their product is less than or equal to the product of their individual norms, expressed as \|$\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}$\| $\leq$ \|$\mathbf{A}$\|\|$\mathbf{B}$\|. This property is a defining characteristic of matrix norms generated by vector norms.
10. State the vector-valued version of the Lagrange Mean Value Theorem. For a continuously partially differentiable function $\mathbf{f}$ on an open convex set E, the theorem states that \|$\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}) - \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{y}$)\| $\leq \max_{t \in [0,1]}$ \|$\mathbf{f}$'($\mathbf{y} + t(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}$))\| $\cdot$ \|$\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}$\|. This provides an upper bound on the change in function value based on the maximum value of the Jacobian matrix norm along the line segment connecting $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$.

Part 4: Glossary of Key Terms

Term	Definition
Absolute Value	A function on real numbers representing distance from zero; it satisfies non-negativity, homogeneity, and the triangle inequality.
Vector Norm	A function |$\cdot|\colon \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ that assigns a non-negative length to vectors, satisfying non-negativity, homogeneity, and the triangle inequality.
p-norm	A general class of vector norms defined by the p-th root of the sum of the absolute values of components raised to the p-th power.
Euclidean Norm (2-norm)	The p-norm where p=2; it represents the standard geometric distance in $\mathbb{R}^n$.
1-norm (Manhattan Norm)	The p-norm where p=1; it is the sum of the absolute values of the vector's components.
Infinity Norm (Maximum Norm)	A vector norm defined as the maximum absolute value among all components of a vector.
CBS Inequality	The Cauchy–Bunyakovsky–Schwarz inequality; states that the squared scalar product of two vectors is $\leq$ the product of their squared Euclidean norms.
Convergence	The property of a sequence where its elements become arbitrarily close to a limit vector as the sequence index increases.
Norm Equivalence	The principle that in $\mathbb{R}^n$, if a sequence converges in one norm, it must converge in all other norms.
Matrix Norm	A function |$\cdot|\colon \mathbb{R}^{n \times n} \to \mathbb{R}$ generated by a vector norm, representing the maximum stretching factor of the matrix.
Supremum (sup)	The least upper bound of a set or function; used in the definition of matrix norms.
Sub-multiplicativity	The matrix norm property where |$\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}| \leq |\mathbf{A}||\mathbf{B}$|.
Cauchy Sequence	A sequence where the distance between any two elements $\mathbf{p}^{(k)}$ and $\mathbf{p}^{(m)}$ becomes arbitrarily small for sufficiently large k and m.
Jacobian Matrix ($\mathbf{f}$')	The matrix of all first-order partial derivatives of a vector-valued function; used in the Lagrange Mean Value Theorem.
Convex Set	A set where the line segment connecting any two points in the set remains entirely within the set.
