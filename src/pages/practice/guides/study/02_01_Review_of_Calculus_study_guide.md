Part 2: Answer Key

1. C[a,b] and $C^m[a,b]: C[a,b$] denotes the set of real-valued functions that are continuous on the closed interval [a,b]. $C^m[a,b$] refers to functions that are continuous on [a,b] and specifically m-times continuously differentiable on the open interval (a,b).
2. Open Interval Notation: The notation $\langle a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n \rangle$ represents an open interval spanned by a set of numbers. It is defined as the interval between the minimum and maximum values of that set, specifically ($\min\{a_1, \ldots, a_n\}, \max\{a_1, \ldots, a_n\}$).
3. Maximum and Minimum Guarantee: Theorem 2.1 states that if a function f is a member of C[a,b], meaning it is continuous on a closed interval, it must have a maximum and minimum within that interval. There will exist points c, d $\in [a,b$] such that f(c) is the maximum and f(d) is the minimum.
4. Intermediate Value Theorem: If f is continuous on [a,b] and f(a) $\neq f(b$), the theorem states that the function takes any value d that lies between f(a) and f(b). Consequently, there must exist some point c in the open interval (a,b) where f(c) = d.
5. Rolle's Theorem Condition: For Rolle's Theorem to apply, a function f must be in $C^1[a,b$] and the values at the endpoints must be equal, such that f(a) = f(b). If these conditions are met, there exists at least one point $\xi$ in the open interval (a,b) where the derivative f'($\xi$) equals zero.
6. Lagrange’s Mean Value Theorem: This theorem states that for a function f $\in C^1[a,b$], there exists a point $\xi \in (a,b$) that satisfies the equation f(b) - f(a) = f'($\xi)(b - a$). This relates the average rate of change over the interval to the instantaneous derivative at an internal point.
7. Taylor’s Theorem $\xi$: In Taylor's Theorem, $\xi$ is a value that depends on x and is located within the open interval $\langle x, x_0 \rangle$ spanned by x and the center point $x_0$. It is used in the final term of the expansion, known as the remainder term, to account for the (n+1)th derivative.
8. Mean Value Theorem for Integrals (g(x)): The function g(x) must be integrable and must not change sign on the interval [a,b]. This means g(x) must be either consistently non-negative (g(x) $\geq 0$) or consistently non-positive (g(x) $\leq 0$) for all x in the interval.
9. Cantor’s Intersection Theorem: The theorem requires a sequence of intervals [$a_n, b_n$] that are closed, bounded, and "nested," meaning [$a_{n+1}, b_{n+1}] \subset [a_n, b_n$]. Additionally, the length of the intervals ($b_n - a_n$) must approach zero as n approaches infinity.
10. Fundamental Theorem of Algebra: The theorem establishes that any polynomial of degree n with complex coefficients (where the leading coefficient is non-zero) has exactly n complex roots. These roots are counted according to their multiplicities.

Part 4: Glossary of Key Terms

Term	Definition
C[a,b]	The set of continuous real-valued functions defined on the closed interval [a,b].
$C^m[a,b$]	The set of real-valued functions that are continuous on [a,b] and m-times continuously differentiable on the open interval (a,b).
$\langle a_1, \ldots, a_n \rangle$	The open interval defined by the minimum and maximum values of the set of numbers \{$a_1, \ldots, a_n$\}.
Cantor’s Intersection Theorem	A theorem stating that a sequence of nested, closed, bounded intervals whose lengths tend to zero converges to a single common point.
Fundamental Theorem of Algebra	A result stating that every nth-degree polynomial with complex coefficients has exactly n complex roots, counting multiplicities.
Intermediate Value Theorem	A theorem asserting that a continuous function on an interval [a,b] takes on every value between f(a) and f(b).
Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem	A theorem relating the change in a function's value over an interval to its derivative at an internal point: f(b) - f(a) = f'($\xi)(b - a$).
Mean Value Theorem for Integrals	A result stating that if f is continuous and g is integrable without a sign change, there exists a point $\xi$ such that $\int_a^b f(x)g(x$) dx = f($\xi) \int_a^b g(x$) dx.
Rolle's Theorem	A special case of the Mean Value Theorem where, if f(a) = f(b), there exists a point $\xi$ in the interval where f'($\xi) = 0$.
Taylor's Theorem	A formula that expresses a function as a sum of terms calculated from the values of its derivatives at a single point, plus a remainder term.
