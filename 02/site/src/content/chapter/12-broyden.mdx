# §2.12 Quasi-Newton Methods · Broyden's Method

> 🚧 **Status:** content stub. **Widget to build:** `BroydenVsNewton` (side-by-side trajectories on the example system, with the live "ratio" column matching Table 2.14). Copy prose from `/home/csd81/Desktop/02/Chapter_2_full.md` §2.12.

Quasi-Newton: $\mathbf{p}^{(k+1)} = \mathbf{p}^{(k)} - (\mathbf{A}^{(k)})^{-1}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{p}^{(k)})$ for some $\mathbf{A}^{(k)} \approx \mathbf{f}'(\mathbf{p}^{(k)})$. Broyden picks the rank-1 update
$$\mathbf{A}^{(k+1)} = \mathbf{A}^{(k)} + \frac{(\mathbf{y}^{(k)} - \mathbf{A}^{(k)}\mathbf{s}^{(k)})(\mathbf{s}^{(k)})^T}{\|\mathbf{s}^{(k)}\|_2^2}, \tag{2.39}$$
which satisfies the secant equation and leaves orthogonal directions untouched. Sherman–Morrison lets us update $(\mathbf{A}^{(k)})^{-1}$ in $O(n^2)$ instead of inverting from scratch.

<TheoremBox kind="theorem" number="2.58" title="Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury">
  Let $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \neq \mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ invertible. Then $\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T$ is invertible iff $1 + \mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{u} \neq 0$, and
  $$(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T)^{-1} = \mathbf{A}^{-1} - \frac{\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{A}^{-1}}{1 + \mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{u}}.$$
</TheoremBox>

Convergence is **superlinear** (no fixed rate; faster than linear but slower than the quadratic rate of full Newton).
